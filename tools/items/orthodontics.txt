# ---- A Growth & Development
F|A|1.4:4.2|How many teeth are in a complete adult natural dentition?|32 teeth, with 16 in each arch.
F|A|1.4:4.2.2.1|How many premolars are there in the adult dentition?|Eight, two in each quadrant. They are sometimes called bicuspids.
F|A|1.4:4.2.2.2|How many molars are normally in each arch?|Six, three in each quadrant: first, second, and third molars.
F|A|1.4:4.3.1|In the Universal numbering system, which tooth is number 1?|The upper right third molar. Numbers 1 through 16 run around the maxillary arch to the upper left third molar.
F|A|1.4:4.8.1|How many lobes does a central incisor develop from, compared to a first molar?|A central incisor develops from four lobes; a first molar develops from five.
F|A|1.4:4.8.2|What are mamelons?|Small rounded projections of enamel on the incisal edges of newly erupted anterior teeth. They wear away soon after eruption.
F|A|1.4:4.5.3|When is cementum formed?|During development of the tooth's root. It is a thin calcified layer covering the root.
F|A|1.5:5.6.1|Describe a Class I occlusion.|Normal profile; the mesiobuccal cusp of the upper first molar falls in the buccal groove of the lower first molar in maximum intercuspation.
F|A|1.5:5.6.2|Describe a Class II occlusion.|Retruded (retrognathic) profile, deficient in chin length; the upper first molar's mesiobuccal cusp falls anterior to the lower first molar's buccal groove.
F|A|1.5:5.6.3|Describe a Class III occlusion.|Protruded (prognathic) profile, excessive chin length; the upper first molar's mesiobuccal cusp falls posterior to the lower first molar's buccal groove.
F|A|1.5:5.6.2.1|What marks a Class II, Division 1 malocclusion?|The maxillary incisors have a normal labiolingual inclination or are too labially inclined.
F|A|1.5:5.6.2.2|What marks a Class II, Division 2 malocclusion?|Two or more maxillary incisors are tipped palatally.
F|A|1.5:5.6.1|What are the normal horizontal and vertical overlap ranges in Class I?|Horizontal overlap 0 to 2 mm; vertical overlap averages 1 to 5 mm.
F|A|1.5:5.6.3|How do the anterior teeth usually meet in a Class III case?|Usually edge to edge, with 0 mm of vertical and horizontal overlap. Negative overlaps are possible.
F|A|1.9:9.21.1|Why are orthodontic casts made before and after treatment?|Before, to explain treatment to the patient; after, to show the amount of change or development that occurred.
F|A|1.9:9.18|What is a space maintainer designed to do?|Preserve the space created by the premature loss of a tooth. It is purely passive.

# ---- B Treatment & Appliances
F|B|1.9:9.1.1|How are orthodontic appliances classified by anchorage?|As fixed or removable, or a combination of both. A patient cannot remove a fixed appliance.
F|B|1.9:9.1.1|How are fixed orthodontic appliances generally anchored?|By metal bands cemented to anchoring teeth.
F|B|1.9:9.1.1|How do most removable appliances get their anchorage?|From acrylic resin bases retained by wrought wire clasps.
F|B|1.9:9.1.2|What does an active orthodontic appliance do?|It moves teeth into a more esthetic and functional alignment, using forces from spring wire attachments or rubber bands.
F|B|1.9:9.1.2|What does a passive orthodontic appliance do?|It holds or maintains teeth in the positions they already occupy.
F|B|1.9:9.1.2|How must a passive appliance's anchorage compare to the teeth it stabilizes?|It must be more resistant to movement than the teeth it is supposed to stabilize.
F|B|1.9:9.15|What are the three parts of a removable orthodontic appliance?|The effecting mechanism, the anchorage, and the retention.
F|B|1.9:9.15.1|What is the effecting mechanism?|The device that moves teeth or holds them in place: active or passive bows with springs, screws, or rubber bands.
F|B|1.9:9.15.3|What provides retention in a removable orthodontic appliance?|Wrought wire clasps embedded in the anchorage and adapted to natural teeth.
F|B|1.9:9.15.3|Which appliance was the forerunner of most removable orthodontic appliances?|The Hawley retainer. Most are modifications of the Hawley principle.
F|B|1.9:9.16.3.2|How do you finish the resin of a Hawley used as a passive retainer?|Leave the resin in contact with the lingual surfaces of the anterior teeth.
F|B|1.9:9.16.3.2|What does an activated labial bow do?|It moves teeth lingually. The resin must be cut back from the anteriors to allow movement.
F|B|1.9:9.17|Why are clear acrylic shields sometimes added to a labial bow?|They add occlusal-gingival stability and help eliminate unwanted rotation of anterior teeth.
F|B|1.9:9.17.3.1|What does the screw in an expansion appliance do when opened?|It expands the dental arch.
F|B|1.9:9.17.4|What does an incisal inclined plane appliance do?|It moves one or two maxillary teeth in crossbite facially, using a resin ramp anchored on at least four lower teeth.
F|B|1.9:9.17.4|What is the maximum angle the inclined plane ramp should make with the occlusal plane?|45 degrees. A steeper ramp moves teeth faster, but should not exceed this.
F|B|1.9:9.17.4|What can happen if an inclined plane appliance separates the teeth too much?|Extreme discomfort in the masticatory muscles or the temporomandibular joints.
F|B|1.9:9.4|When is a preformed stainless steel crown used instead of a band?|When the natural tooth is badly broken down and needs to be covered to retard its deterioration.
F|B|1.9:9.13|How does the lab usually receive bands for a fixed appliance?|The dentist fits the bands, makes an impression with them in place, and seats them in the impression before sending it.
F|B|1.9:9.20.1|What is the space-closing appliance used for?|With rubber bands, it gives a degree of bodily tooth movement while closing a space.
