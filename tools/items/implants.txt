F|A|2.6:6.1.2|What is a dental implant?|A prosthetic device placed within bone to provide retention and support for fixed or removable appliances.
F|A|2.6:6.1.2|What can implants be used to replace or retain?|Single teeth, multiple teeth, or dentures.
F|A|2.6:6.1.3|Whose directions should be followed closely when fabricating implant prostheses?|The manufacturer's directions for the implant system being used.
F|A|2.6:6.2|What is the implant fixture?|The permanent device implanted into bone after the dentist drills a hole at the proper location and angle.
F|A|2.6:6.2|What is the most common material for implant fixtures?|Titanium, an inert material.
F|A|2.6:6.2|What is the purpose of the cover screw?|It seals the fixture opening during healing so bone or tissue does not grow into it while the fixture osseointegrates.
F|A|2.6:6.2|What is the abutment?|The part that attaches to the fixture and supports and retains the restorative components, running from bone level through the tissue.
F|A|2.6:6.2|Where does an abutment normally end?|At or slightly above the gingival tissue.

F|A|2.6:6.3.2|What may help the dentist decide whether enough bone is available for fixtures?|A radiographic stent.
F|A|2.6:6.3.3|Why is a surgical template fabricated?|To help the surgeon place the implants in the proper location.
F|A|2.6:6.3.4|What is done in the first surgical procedure?|The implant fixtures are placed into the bone with the cover screw.
F|A|2.6:6.3.5|What is done in the second surgical procedure?|The abutment and healing cap are placed.
F|A|2.6:6.4.1|What is osseointegration?|The direct bone anchorage of an inert material, usually titanium, that provides a foundation to support the prosthesis.
F|A|2.6:6.4.2|What forms on titanium when an implant is first inserted?|A layer of oxide.

F|A|2.6:6.4.3|Which jaw usually heals faster around implants, and why?|The mandible, because it is mostly harder compact cortical bone rather than softer cancellous bone.
F|A|2.6:6.4.4|How should occlusal forces on implants be directed?|More vertically than laterally, to reduce traumatic failure.
F|A|2.6:6.4.4|What is an advantage of osseointegration?|Occlusal forces are transmitted directly to the bone.
